Chapter 3 — Innovation and Growth

Growth in a model with variety of intermediate goods

Introduction

Context

From the AK model to variety of goods

In the ”AK” model, positive growth comes from externalities that excatly compensate marginal decreasing return of capital at the firm level.

At the aggregate level, marginal return in capital is constant (does not depend on $K$) and there is no exhaustion of growth (as it is the case in the neo-classical growth model).

$$\eta+\alpha=1$$

However, the result relies on strong assumption $(\eta+\alpha=1)$.

In the model with variety of goods, long-term economic growth comes from R&D efforts to increase the number of intermediate goods available for the production of the final good.

A model with more than one production sector

Models with variety of goods are complex: one needs to describe the functionning of more than one production sectors.

1

Growth in this model is driven by technological change that arises from intentional investment decisions made by profit-maximizing agents.

2

The distinguishing feature of the technology as an input is that it is neither a conventional good nor a public good; it is a nonrival, partially excludable good.

3

Because of the nonconvexity introduced by a nonrival good, price-taking competition cannot be supported. Instead, the equilibrium is one with monopolistic competition.

The main conclusions

▶ the stock of human capital determines the rate of growth
▶ too little human capital is devoted to research in equilibrium
▶ integration into world markets will increase growth rates
▶ having a large population is not sufficient to generate growth

Framework

Building blocks of the model

A simplified version of the model. (No Capital goods, and constant share of labour in research sector)

3 production sectors in the economy:

Final output sectoruses intermediate goods and labor; output: final good; perfect competition
→
Intermediate good sectorinput: final good; output: intermediate goods; monopolistic competition
→
Research sectordevelops new variety of intermediate goods; uses labor; perfect competition

Househods sector

1 representative household that maximises its intemporal utility function (no desutility of labor)

Households and production

Households

Maximisation program

$$\mathrm{Max}=\int_0^\infty \frac{c(t)^{1-\varepsilon}-1}{1-\varepsilon}e^{-\rho t}dt$$

Wealth accumulation equation:

$$\frac{dS(t)}{dt}=r(t)S(t)+w(t)L(t)-c(t)$$

$L$ constant (no population growth, no labour desutility in the utility function), and the household takes $w(t)$ and $r(t)$ as exogenous.

We will consider $r(t)=r$. One could consider a time varying interest rate $r(t)$. Model resolution leads to $r(t)=r$.

The transversality condition

$$\lim_{t\to\infty} S(t)e^{-rt}=0$$

On the optimal growth path

$$g_C=\frac{r-\rho}{\varepsilon}$$
Final good sector

Production function

$$Y_t=L_1^{1-\alpha}\int_0^{M_t}x_i^\alpha di$$

where $Y$ is the value added in the final good sector, $x_i$ the quantity of input $i$ used by the final good sector.

Input are indexed by $i$ over $(0,M_t)$ with $M_t$ the measure of variety of intermediate input (a higher $M_t$ = more intermediate goods).

$L_1$ is the fixed quantity of labour input in the final good sector.

Intermediate good sector

Production and GDP

The production of 1 unit of intermediate input requires 1 unit of final good.

$$x_i=x_i$$

$X_t$ the total amount of final good required to product all intermediate goods.

$$X_t=\int_0^{M_t}x_i\,di$$

GDP

$$GDP_t=Y_t-X_t$$

and (because there is no capital good, so no investment)

$$C_t=GDP_t$$

and

$$g_C=g_Y$$
Perfect competition

Price of intermediate input and wages

Perfect competition in the final good sector ⇒ prices of labor and intermediate input are equal to their marginal productivity.

$$p_i=L_1^{1-\alpha}\alpha x_i^{\alpha-1}$$

$p_i$ = price (in term of final good price = 1) of input $i$

$$w_t=(1-\alpha)L_1^{-\alpha}\int_0^{M_t}x_i^\alpha di$$

Intermediate goods and variety

Monopolistic competition

Profit maximisation in the intermediate good sector

Monopolistic competition ⇒ each firm chooses the quantity $x_i$ that, at the given price $p_i$, maximises its profit

$$\Pi_i=p_ix_i-x_i=\alpha L_1^{1-\alpha}x_i^\alpha-x_i$$

Profit maximisation (with respect to $x_i$) leads to

$$x_i=L_1\alpha^{2/(1-\alpha)}$$

Profit of firm $i$

$$\Pi_i=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}L_1\alpha^{2/(1-\alpha)}$$

and doesn’t depend on $i$ (all entreprises produce the same quantity of their product and consume the same amount of final good as intermediate consumption)

$$x_i=\frac{X_t}{M_t}=x$$
Variety

Economic growth and the variety of intermediate inputs

$$Y_t=L_1^{1-\alpha}\int_0^{M_t}x_i^\alpha di$$
$$Y_t=L_1^{1-\alpha}\int_0^{M_t}(X_t/M_t)^\alpha di$$
$$Y_t=L_1^{1-\alpha}M_t^{1-\alpha}X_t^\alpha$$

for a given value of $X_t$, $Y$ is a increasing function of $M_t$.

$$Y_t=M_t^{1-\alpha}L_1^{1-\alpha}X_t^\alpha$$

Using $X_t=M_tx_t$ leads to

$$Y_t=M_tL_1^{1-\alpha}x^\alpha$$

and

$$GDP_t=M_t\left(L_1^{1-\alpha}x^\alpha-x\right)$$

Growth and variety

$Y$ and $GDP$ and $C$ grow at the same rate as the measure of intermediate good variety $M_t$.

Research sector

New varieties of intermediate goods

Assumption: the growth in $M_t$ depends of efforts in R&D ($L_2$ the amount of labour in the research sector)

$$\frac{dM_t}{dt}=\lambda M_tL_2$$

Note: there is here a positive externality of research.

Research, interest rate and growth

Research sector

Profit in the research sector

Each new intermediate good leads to a flow of profit by unit of time

$$\Pi=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}L_1\alpha^{2/(1-\alpha)}$$

The value of each innovation is the discounted sum of profits it can generate:

$$\frac{\Pi}{r}$$

The profit in the research sector is:

$$\frac{\Pi}{r}\frac{dM_t}{dt}-w_tL_2$$
$$\frac{\Pi}{r}\lambda M_tL_2-w_tL_2$$

The research sector is in perfect competition (free entry), the profit is null.

This leads to:

$$r=\lambda M_t\Pi/w_t$$
Interest rate

Interest rate

using:

$$w_t=(1-\alpha)L_1^{-\alpha}\int_0^{M_t}x_i^\alpha di$$
$$x_i=\frac{X_t}{M_t}=x$$
$$x=L_1\alpha^{2/(1-\alpha)}$$

leads to

$$w_t=(1-\alpha)\alpha^{2\alpha/(1-\alpha)}M_t$$

hence

$$r=\lambda M_t\Pi/w_t=\alpha\lambda L_1$$
Growth rate

The growth rate of the economy

Up to now, we have shown that:

GDP grow at the same rate as $M_t$
$$g=\lambda L_2$$
Gowth rate of consumption is
$$g_C=\frac{r-\rho}{\varepsilon}$$
Interest rate is such that
$$r=\lambda M_t\Pi/w_t=\alpha\lambda L_1$$

Using labor market equilibrium

$$L_1+L_2=L$$

Leads to

$$\boxed{g=\frac{\alpha\lambda L-\rho}{\alpha+\varepsilon}}$$

Scale effect

Growth increases with productivity in the research sector and the number of worker in the economy $L$ (but without research $\lambda=0$ $L$ has no impact on the growth rate of the economy).

The model predicts the existence of a scale effect: the larger the population size, the higher the growth rate.

Main conclusions

Summing up

1

Economic growth comes from the increase in the variety of intermediate input.

2

Long run growth is possible because of the constant marginal return of the variety of intermediate input.

3

Openness to trade is good for growth: (i) increase in $M$ via trade; (ii) increase in $M$ via higher R&D (because of higher profits).

4

There is neither absolute nor conditional convergence.

5

Decentralized economy is not optimal (because of imperfect competition).

6

Tax policy can bring decentralized economy to the optimal economy (...more on that at the end of the lecture).

$$g=\frac{\alpha\lambda L-\rho}{\alpha+\varepsilon}.$$

If the two countries merge and become a unique economy, what will be its growth rate of output?

$$M_0=M_0^*$$

(time zero denotes the moment trade starts).

Suppose, in addition, that the two economies produce, before trade, disjoint subsets of intermediate goods (specialization).

When trade is introduced, each monopolist can therefore sell its product in two markets.

Economic policy

Economic Policy in an economy with variety of goods

Solving the centralised economy problem leads to a higher growth rate that that achieved in the decentralized economy.

How to replicate the centralized economy outcome in a decentralized one ?

Subsidizing intermediate goods

subsidizing the purchase of intermediate goods at a rate such that their effective price is equal to their production cost

financed by a lump sum tax on households (no distorsion of their consumption / saving arbitrage)

Subsidizing final goods

subsidizing the production of final goods at a rate such that the demand for intermediate goods is equal to the demand that would prevail if the price of intermediate goods were equal to their production cost,

financing this subsidy by a lump-sum tax on households,
